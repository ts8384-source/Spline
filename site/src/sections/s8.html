Your idea: instead of one position per frame, the network outputs a handful of control points, and a fixed matrix turns them into the next W frames: . Here the "network" is an oracle: I fit the best spline to the true future, so you see the best the readout could ever do. Slide "now" along the ball's path.

Top: true path (grey), the window (shaded), the fitted spline (yellow) and its control points (dots, joined by the control polygon). Lower left: the readout matrix : each row is a future frame, each column a control point, brightness is that point's weight (the bump functions of a B-spline, as a table). Lower right: best-possible error for every "now", with bands where the window contains a bounce. Turn on the corner knot to see what it costs (3 extra control points per bounce) and what it needs (knowing when the bounce happens).
