2 · Chain the pieces: the slope match is built in

Put several knots in a row. Each knot has a value and a slope, and each piece between two knots is a Hermite piece from slide 1. Because the piece on the left of a knot and the piece on the right of it are both given the same value and the same slope there, they always agree on both: the curve is C¹ without any solving. What nothing forces is the bend (the second derivative), so it can jump at a knot. Drag the knots, and in "free" mode (the start) drag the round ends of the arrows to tilt the slope at a knot. The other two modes choose the slopes for you and lock the arrows.

Top: the curve, the knots (dots) and the tangent arrows. Lower: the bend along the curve. Inside a piece it is a straight line; at a knot it can jump (grey dashed). Choose the third tangent rule and the jumps disappear: those tangents are exactly what the ordinary smooth cubic spline of the Math page uses, so an interpolating cubic spline is a Hermite spline whose tangents were chosen to make it C². Hermite keeps the tangents as free numbers instead, which is more freedom and a little less smoothness.
