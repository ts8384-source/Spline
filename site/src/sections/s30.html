3 · Training without peeking

The network may only see frames up to "now", yet it must forecast the next W frames, and you need a loss. The way out is time: a forecast made at frame covers frames , and each of those frames arrives later. Nothing is peeked at; you grade the forecast when its answer shows up. Below, each cell of the grid is one pair (forecast made at , target frame ). Green cells already have their answer, grey ones are still waiting. Pick a way to build the loss and see which cells it uses.

Top: the true path (solid up to now, faint after: the network never sees that part) and forecast splines. Grid: horizontal = target frame, vertical = the frame the forecast was made at. The forecasts here are a stand-in: the best spline fit plus a made-up error that grows with lead time. They show the bookkeeping, not a trained network. The "which cells to score" rules are standard multi-step-ahead training bookkeeping; the note about gradients is my reasoning and I have not tested it on your network.
