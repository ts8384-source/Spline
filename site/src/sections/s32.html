12 · What you choose: degree, knots and basis rule

Slide 11 let you move the learned part, the control points . Here you keep moving them, and you also turn the knobs that are fixed in a trained model. The number of control points stays 7: it is set by the other choices (), so it is not a knob. The thin grey curve is the default for these same points (cubic, evenly spaced knots, B-spline), so you can see what each knob changes.

What a knot is. The curve is made of cubic pieces glued end to end. A knot is the seam: the clock time where one piece hands over to the next. On the curve above, each seam is marked with a yellow ring and its smoothness. Drag a white handle below to slide a seam along the clock. Pile all 3 seams on one spot and the curve is allowed to turn sharply there, because it no longer has to match slope and bend across the seam. That is the "corner" a bounce needs.

Top: the curve for the current choices. Lower picture: the bumps that those choices produce, and the inner knots as white handles on the baseline: drag them sideways. A number like ×3 means that many knots sit at the same place. The ends carry knots each (clamped), which is why the curve starts and ends exactly on the first and last control point.
