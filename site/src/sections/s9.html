7 · Joining two pieces: continuity

A spline is several pieces joined end to end. At the join, how much must the two pieces agree? Two cubic pieces share the white join point. The yellow and blue arrows are the velocity each piece has at the join. The short lines along the curves are a curvature comb: the longer the line, the tighter the bend there. Drag the points, then try the locks.

: the pieces touch, but the path can have a corner, so the two arrows point different ways. : the arrows coincide, meaning same direction and the same speed, so the clock matters here too. With a shared join this forces Q1 to be the mirror image of P2 across J, so Q1 stops being free. : the comb is continuous across the join, which also ties down Q2. Each lock removes one free control point: 7, then 6, then 5. Five is exactly what a cubic B-spline with two pieces uses (number of pieces + 3).

Matching only the direction of the tangent, not the speed, is a weaker condition (usually written G¹). It looks smooth but isn't C¹.
