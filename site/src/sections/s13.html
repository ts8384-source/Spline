11 · Knots and the B-spline basis

A spline curve is a weighted sum of bumps, and the knots decide where the bumps sit. The knots are the values of where one piece hands over to the next, written as a list called the knot vector. Top: the bumps. Each bump has its own colour and number. Click a chip to isolate one (click again to release). Drag the yellow knots along the bottom edge (click one to select it). Bottom: each bump scaled by its weight (faint) and their sum, the curve (white). Drag the numbered weights.

Degree: slide it from 0 to 3 and watch the bumps being built: boxes (0), triangles (1), rounded hats (2), the smooth cubic bump (3). Each bump is a blend of two bumps one degree lower, which is all the recursion below says. Copies: repeat the selected knot and the bumps pinch toward it, so the curve is allowed to bend more sharply there. At a knot with m copies the curve is smooth to order p − m: for a cubic that is C², C¹, C⁰ for 1, 2, 3 copies. With p + 1 copies the curve may even jump. Whatever you do, the bumps add up to 1 everywhere, which is why a weighted sum of them is an average of the weights.
